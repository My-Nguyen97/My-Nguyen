# Perfect Competition – Engagement Brief

## The Problem

The farm needs to decide how many beds of tomatoes, carrots, and mesclun to plant to make the most profit during the growing season. The farm has 64 available planting beds, limited labor, and different costs for each crop. Since the farm operates in a competitive market, it cannot control the selling prices.

The main challenge is figuring out which combination of crops will produce the highest profit without using too much labor or exceeding the available space. Planting more of one crop could increase revenue, but it can also increase costs.

## What I Am Assuming

I am assuming that the selling prices, growing season, and planting limits will remain the same. I also assume that the farm can hire temporary workers if additional labor is needed.

Another important assumption is that labor costs will increase as more beds of the same crop are planted. This means that even though tomatoes generate more revenue per bed, planting too many tomatoes could eventually become too expensive.

## Hypothesis

I predict that the best combination will be **15 tomato beds, 20 carrot beds, and 25 mesclun beds**, for a total of 60 beds.

I chose this combination because tomatoes generate the most revenue per bed, but they also have the highest increase in labor requirements. Carrots and mesclun produce less revenue but have lower labor requirements and slower diminishing returns.

I believe this combination could provide a good balance between earning revenue and controlling operating costs. Leaving four beds unused might also be more profitable than planting additional crops if the cost of labor becomes too high.

## How I Would Know I Was Wrong

My hypothesis would be wrong if the Excel Solver model finds a different crop combination that generates more profit while meeting the farm's constraints.

For example, if planting 20 tomato beds instead of 15 produces more profit, my prediction would be incorrect. I would compare the model's results with my original prediction to understand how labor costs, diminishing returns, and available land affect profitability.
